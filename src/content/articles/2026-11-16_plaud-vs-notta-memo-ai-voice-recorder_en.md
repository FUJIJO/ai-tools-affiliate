# Plaud vs Notta Memo: Which AI Voice Recorder Fits a Busy Schedule? (2026)

I am a working dad who blogs on the side, and my biggest productivity leak is not writing. It is forgetting. Meeting details slip away while I take notes, and blog ideas I have on the commute are gone by bedtime. An AI voice recorder promises to fix that: press a button, talk, and get a transcript and summary later.

This guide compares two devices that have affiliate programs on A8.net, Plaud and Notta Memo. Every price and spec below comes from the official Japan stores and product pages, checked on 16 November 2026 (JST). This is a comparison of official information, not a long-term hands-on review.

A note for readers outside Japan: prices are in Japanese yen, tax included, from the Japanese stores. Plaud also sells internationally, and prices and plans there may differ. A8.net is a Japan-focused affiliate network, so the links below are mainly for readers in Japan.

## The short version

- **Try your phone first.** Notta's free app plan gives 120 minutes of transcription a month (3 minutes per recording). If that already solves your problem, you do not need hardware.
- **Want the most free minutes?** Notta Memo includes a free "Hardware Plan" with 600 minutes a month.
- **Care about battery life or wearing it?** Plaud includes a free Starter plan with 300 minutes a month, and its NotePin S clips onto your clothes.

Two questions settle most of the decision: how many minutes you record per month, and whether you need to record phone calls.

## What it costs in year one

Your monthly recording time matters more than the sticker price.

- **About 1 hour of meetings a week (~240 min/month):** both free tiers cover it. Year one is just the device.
- **About 2–2.5 hours a week (~500–600 min/month):** Notta Memo's free tier still covers it. Plaud needs the Pro plan, so a Plaud Note plus one year of Pro is JPY 44,300.
- **3+ hours a week (720+ min/month):** both need a paid plan. Budget roughly JPY 14,220/year for Notta Premium or JPY 16,800/year for Plaud Pro.

Add up one month of the meetings you actually want recorded before you buy. That alone avoids most wasted subscriptions.

## Plaud: battery life and hands-free recording

Plaud makes card-style recorders (Plaud Note, Plaud Note Pro) and wearables (Plaud NotePin, NotePin S). Its official pages describe transcription in 112 languages, speaker labels, and summary templates.

- **Plaud Note (JPY 27,500):** card-sized, attaches to the back of a phone with a MagSafe-compatible case, switches manually between call and in-person recording.
- **Plaud Note Pro (JPY 30,800):** switches modes automatically, has a display, and lists a pickup range of up to 5 m on the official comparison page.
- **Plaud NotePin S (JPY 28,600):** ships with a clip, magnetic pin, neck strap, and wristband. The official comparison lists it as in-person recording only, so it cannot record phone calls.

The Japan store also lists a "Plaud Note Pro Flow Edition" at JPY 29,700. I could not confirm how it differs from the standard Note Pro, so check the product page before you choose it.

According to Plaud, the free Plaud Desktop app can record online meetings without adding a bot to the call. The Japan store advertises a 30-day return guarantee and a one-year product warranty.

**Pros**

- Long official battery figures mean fewer charging chores.
- The NotePin S suits standing conversations and notes on the move.

**Cons**

- 300 free minutes a month runs out fast if you have many meetings.
- No call recording on the NotePin S.

## Notta Memo: the most free minutes

Notta Memo comes from Notta, the Japanese transcription service. According to Notta's announcement, since 18 September 2026 buyers get a free Hardware Plan with 600 minutes a month, up from 300.

Official specs: about 28 g, about 30 hours of recording, 32 GB of storage, and a physical switch between call mode and meeting mode. Notta's help center says the Hardware Plan allows up to 5 hours per recording and 100 AI summaries a month.

The **Notta Memo Pro (JPY 29,700)** detects calls versus in-person conversations automatically, charges over USB-C, connects to a PC over USB, and syncs recordings to Notta automatically when you return to a trusted Wi-Fi network.

**Pros**

- 600 free minutes a month covers many people without a subscription.
- If you already use the Notta app, everything stays in one place.

**Cons**

- Notta's own pages disagree on what happens if you already pay for Notta: the product FAQ says the Hardware Plan does not apply, while the help center says Premium users get an extra 600 minutes. If you are a paying Notta user, ask support before buying.
- Return terms depend on whether you buy from Notta's store or Amazon.

## A low-effort way to choose

1. **Need phone calls?** Pick Notta Memo, Notta Memo Pro, Plaud Note, or Plaud Note Pro. Skip the NotePin S.
2. **Under 300 minutes a month?** Any of them works. Between 300 and 600, Notta Memo has the edge. Above that, compare the paid plans.
3. **Want to wear it?** Plaud NotePin S.

## Using it as a side-hustle idea capture tool

What I want most is a voice notebook for blog ideas: talk for a minute or two on a walk, then turn the transcript into a rough outline later. Transcripts and AI summaries still contain mistakes, so check numbers and names against the recording or official sources before you publish anything.

## Recording etiquette

Plaud's own comparison page asks you to tell participants and get consent before recording. For work meetings, also check whether company policy allows recording and cloud storage.

## FAQ

### Isn't a phone app enough?

For many people, yes. Notta's free app plan gives 120 minutes a month (3 minutes per recording). Dedicated hardware mainly adds one-button recording and call capture from the back of your phone.

### Will I have to pay monthly?

Not necessarily. According to the official pages, Plaud includes 300 minutes and Notta Memo includes 600 minutes a month with the device. Beyond that you need a paid plan.

### Will this boost my side income?

Not by itself. It reduces the effort of capturing ideas. You still have to write and publish, and results vary.

## Final take

An AI voice recorder hands off the note-taking, which is exactly what a lazy-but-busy person needs. It is still a JPY 20,000–30,000 purchase, so decide how many minutes you record and whether you need calls first.
