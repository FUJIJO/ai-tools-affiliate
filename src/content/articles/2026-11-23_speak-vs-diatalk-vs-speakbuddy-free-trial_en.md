# Speak vs DiaTalk vs SpeakBuddy: Which AI English App's Free Trial Fits a Busy Schedule? (Japan, 2026)

I'm Takeru, a self-confessed lazy dad who writes about money and productivity on the side. Every few months I decide to "finally get serious about English" — usually right after a work email in English lands in my inbox, or when I want to read a new AI tool's docs without a translator. Then I remember I have no time for classes and zero energy to book online lessons.

AI English conversation apps fix the scheduling problem: no booking, no tutor waiting, just you and an AI partner for a few minutes. But for a lazy person, the real risk isn't the lessons. It's **starting a "free" trial, forgetting about it, and getting billed for a full year.**

So this comparison of three apps popular in Japan — **Speak**, **SpeakBuddy**, and **DiaTalk** — focuses on the boring-but-expensive stuff: trial length, whether you're billed automatically afterward, the cancellation deadline, and price.

To be upfront: this is based on what each company's official site, help center, and FAQ said on November 23, 2026 (JST) — not a long-term hands-on review. Results vary from person to person, and no app guarantees you'll become fluent or earn more from your job or side hustle.

**Who this is for:** people living in Japan who are learning English (including Japanese speakers who read English). All prices below are the Japanese plans in yen, tax included. SpeakBuddy and DiaTalk are Japanese services built for Japanese speakers. Speak is available in many countries, but prices and trials vary by region.

**About the affiliate link:** the DiaTalk link is through A8.net, a Japanese affiliate network. A8.net programs are aimed at Japan, so the reward conditions may not apply to sign-ups from elsewhere. The Speak and SpeakBuddy links are plain official links.

## Quick verdict

- **You know you'll forget to cancel** → SpeakBuddy. Its official FAQ says that when the 3-day free trial ends, you simply become a free user. There's no automatic switch to a paid plan, so you don't need to cancel anything.
- **You want the most speaking practice for the least money** → Speak. 7-day free trial, and the cheapest plan of the three when paid yearly. But if you don't cancel at least 24 hours before the trial ends, you're charged the **annual** price of the plan you picked.
- **You want a fixed 10-minute daily habit focused on work English** → DiaTalk. You get one 10-minute conversation credit every morning. The 7-day trial requires a credit card and renews automatically.

Whichever one you try: **put the cancellation deadline in your calendar the minute you sign up.**

## Three checks before you start any trial

1. **Will it bill me automatically?** SpeakBuddy: no. Speak and DiaTalk: yes.
2. **When exactly is the deadline?** Speak: at least 24 hours (one day) before the trial ends. DiaTalk (web): by the trial end date, through its cancellation form.
3. **What gets charged?** Speak's official blog says that after the trial you're billed the annual price of the plan you chose. DiaTalk's 12-month plan is also paid up front.

My personal rule: the moment I start a trial, I add a reminder two days before the deadline that says "keep it or cancel it?"

## Speak: the most practice for the money

Speak teaches phrases through short videos with native-speaking teachers, then has you practice out loud with AI. Its Japan FAQ says AI conversation volume isn't limited on either plan.

Per Speak's official Japanese blog (prices as of Oct 21, 2026, tax included):

- **Premium:** ¥3,800/month or ¥19,800/year (¥1,650/month). Review lessons generated from your mistakes are capped at 3 per day.
- **Premium Plus:** ¥5,800/month or ¥29,800/year (~¥2,483/month). Adds **Phone Speak** (AI "phone calls," up to 15 per month, up to 10 minutes each), unlimited review lessons, a "Mistakes" drill, Custom Courses (beta), and discussion courses.

Phone Speak launched on October 20, 2026. It's hands-free, and you can ask questions in Japanese when you get stuck. Premium users get one trial call. On November 5, Speak also announced a "Live Tutor Lesson" built on OpenAI's GPT-Live-1 voice model, currently rolling out to some users first, with plans to expand to Premium and Premium Plus.

There's also a first-time Premium Plus offer: one month for ¥180, which then rolls into the **annual** Premium Plus plan. And there's a free web lesson you can try without creating an account.

**Pros**

- Cheapest of the three on a yearly plan (¥1,650/month for Premium)
- No stated limit on AI conversation
- Actively shipping new speaking features

**Cons**

- Miss the 24-hour cancellation window and you're billed a full year
- Monthly billing is in-app only
- Speak says Phone Speak is shown in the app as best for intermediate learners and up

Official site: [Speak (Japan)](https://www.speak.com/jp) (not an affiliate link)

## SpeakBuddy: the no-stress first step

SpeakBuddy has you chat with AI characters across 1,000+ scenes (daily life, travel, business), in sessions from about 5 minutes. According to its FAQ, each lesson combines vocabulary, listening, conversation, writing, and applied practice.

Pricing: ¥3,980 for one month or ¥28,400 for 12 months (about ¥2,367/month). Every plan includes all features and content.

The lazy-person win: **the free trial doesn't turn into a subscription on its own.** If you don't buy a plan during the 3 days, you become a free user and the paid features lock. There's nothing to cancel. The trial is one per person.

**Pros**

- No auto-billing after the trial
- Structured curriculum that also covers vocabulary, listening, and writing
- Free spoken level-check test

**Cons**

- Shortest trial (3 days)
- Paid plans do auto-renew. Cancel in the app (Menu → Contact → cancellation option), not by email
- No refunds for purchased periods, in principle. Billing goes through App Store / Google Play

Official site: [SpeakBuddy](https://app.speakbuddy.me/) (not an affiliate link)

## DiaTalk: a 10-minute habit for work English

DiaTalk is made by PROGRIT, a Japanese English-coaching company. After each AI conversation, it analyzes what you said and suggests better phrasing. You can save phrases and track how many words you spoke. It offers business-scene training, adjustable difficulty, speed, and tempo, and a Japanese help feature.

The credit system is what makes it interesting for busy people: **one 10-minute conversation credit is added every morning at 3:00 a.m. JST.** You can store up to 7 and use as many as you like in a day, so missed weekdays can be made up on the weekend.

Pricing (tax included): ¥4,380 for one month, or ¥39,400 for 12 months paid up front (≈¥3,283/month, 25% off monthly).

First-time sign-ups get 7 days' worth of free trial. On the web, you have to pick a plan and enter a credit card. The trial auto-renews, and to cancel you submit the cancellation form by the trial's end date. DiaTalk's legal notice says it doesn't accept cancellations for customer convenience except where required by law.

**Pros**

- Built-in "10 minutes a day" rhythm
- Feedback and review after every conversation
- Business-focused scenarios

**Cons**

- Most expensive of the three
- Card required for the trial, and it auto-renews
- The site showed a campaign countdown I couldn't read the details of. Trial terms for the 12-month plan may differ, so check at sign-up

## Fitting English into a dad schedule

- **Commute or chores:** hands-free practice (like Speak's Phone Speak) means no screen needed.
- **10 minutes after the kids are asleep:** DiaTalk's fixed 10-minute sessions make it easy to stop guilt-free.
- **Weekends:** catch up on stored credits or review lessons.

For a side hustle, English mainly helps indirectly: reading new AI tools' docs and updates in the original, or checking English materials yourself. An English app won't pay you directly, so treat it as an investment of your time.

You can also practice with the voice mode of general AI assistants like ChatGPT, but you have to set up the corrections and review yourself. If even deciding what to practice feels like work, a dedicated app that bundles lessons and review is easier to stick with.

## FAQ

**Can I use only the free trial?**
Yes. SpeakBuddy doesn't auto-bill. For Speak, cancel at least 24 hours before the trial ends. For DiaTalk (web), submit the cancellation form by the trial end date.

**Which is cheapest?**
On yearly plans, Speak Premium (¥19,800/year = ¥1,650/month). On monthly plans: Speak Premium ¥3,800, SpeakBuddy ¥3,980, DiaTalk ¥4,380.

**I live outside Japan. Does this apply to me?**
Partly. The prices and trials here are the Japanese ones. SpeakBuddy and DiaTalk are built for Japanese speakers, and the A8.net link is Japan-focused. Check Speak's terms in your own region.

**Will an AI app make me fluent?**
No one can promise that. DiaTalk itself notes that results vary. Pick the one whose routine you'll actually keep.
